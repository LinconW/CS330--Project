#CS330 Portfolio
## How do I approach designing software?

My approach to designing software has become more structured throughout this course. While working on my 3D scene, I learned how important it is to break a larger visual idea into smaller components such as objects, textures, lighting, camera controls, and transformations. I followed an iterative design process where I first created the basic geometry and scene layout, then gradually added movement, textures, materials, and lighting. This helped me avoid trying to complete everything at once. In future projects, I can use the same strategy of separating a large problem into smaller, testable parts and improving each component individually before combining everything into the final product.

## How do I approach developing programs?

While developing my 3D scene, I used several strategies that were newer to me, especially working with OpenGL, shaders, textures, lighting, and three-dimensional transformations. Iteration played a major role in the project because the scene changed throughout each milestone. I frequently tested the program, identified problems, adjusted values or code, and ran it again to see the results. For example, positioning objects, adjusting the camera, and applying textures required multiple attempts before they looked correct. My development approach has evolved because I now spend more time testing individual parts of a program instead of making many changes at once. The milestones also helped me understand how a complex graphics program can be built gradually from a simple foundation into a complete 3D environment.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics and visualization gave me experience with concepts that I had not worked with extensively before, including 3D coordinate systems, transformations, rendering, lighting, textures, and user-controlled cameras. These skills will help me in future computer science courses because they strengthened my understanding of mathematics, debugging, object-oriented programming, and how different parts of a larger software system interact.

Professionally, this project also gave me more experience working with an existing codebase and adding functionality to it over time. Even if I do not specifically pursue a career in computer graphics, the problem-solving and development skills from this course can transfer to software engineering and other areas of computer science. Building the completed scene showed me how planning, iteration, testing, and debugging can turn an initial concept into a working software product.
